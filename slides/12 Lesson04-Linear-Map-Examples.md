Just to show that vectorspaces and linear maps are not just on $\mathbb{R}^n$ we look at another example.

Consider the vectorspace

$$V=C^2[a,b]$$

of continuously two times differentiable functions on the interval $[a,b]$. And the vectorspace 
$$
W=C[a,b]
$$
of continuous functions on the same interval. These can both be made into vectorspaces in the usual manner

Consider the map 
$$D:C^2[a,b] \to C[a,b]$$ 
defined simply by differentiation. That is the $D$ maps a function to its derivative. Is this a linear map ?
