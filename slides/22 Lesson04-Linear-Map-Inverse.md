The following property holds only for linear maps.

### Injectivity

Injectivity $\Leftrightarrow \operatorname{ker} (L)=\{ \mathbf{0} \}$

<div class="h3-blue">
Proof
</div>
Assumme $\operatorname{ker}(L)=\{ \mathbf{0} \}$

Let $\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2 \in V$ and assume $\operatorname{ker} (L)=\{\mathbf{0} \}$ then

$$
L (\mathbf{v}_1)=L (\mathbf{v}_2)\Rightarrow L (\mathbf{v}_1-\mathbf{v}_2)=\mathbf{0} \Rightarrow \mathbf{v}_1-\mathbf{v}_2=\mathbf{0} \Rightarrow \mathbf{v}_1=\mathbf{v}_2
$$

Conversely, assume $L$ is injective. And consider any $\mathbf{v}\in \operatorname{ker} (L)$, then
$$
L (\mathbf{v})=L (\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0} \Rightarrow \mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}
$$

that is $\operatorname{ker} (L)=\{ \mathbf{0}\}$
