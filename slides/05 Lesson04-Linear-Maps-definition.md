<h3 class="h3-blue">Definition: Linear map</h3>

Let $V$ and $W$ be two vectorspaces over the same field $\mathbb{F}$. A map

$L:V\rightarrow W$

is said to be linear if

$\forall s\in \mathbb{F},\forall \mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2 \in V$

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(\mathbf{v}_1+\mathbf{v}_2)&=L(\mathbf{v}_1)+L(\mathbf{v}_2)\\
L(s\mathbf{v}_1)&=sL(\mathbf{v}_1)
\end{aligned}
$$

<br>

Notice that the scalar multiplication and addition of vectors are in two entirely different vectorspaces
