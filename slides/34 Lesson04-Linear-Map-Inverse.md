title: Inverse of linear maps on the same space
template: default
---

Consider finite dimensional vectorspaces $V$ with dimension $n$.

Consider a linear map $L:V \to V$

In this case we have the notion of a determinant of the map $L$ which is a scalar quantity that is independent of basis.

And it is defined as the determinant of the matrix representation of $L$ in any basis.

To be invertible we must have $\operatorname{Ker} (L)={\mathbf{0}}$

And from this it follows that $\operatorname{Range} (L)=V$
by the rank-nullity theorem. And therefore it is invertible

But concluding that the kernel of $L$ consists of only $\mathbf{0}$ can be determined by the **determinant** of the
map $L$. And this is **independent of basis**

So, we can calculate it in any coordinates by our previous definition

And the condition is
$$
\operatorname{det} (L)\neq 0 \Leftrightarrow \operatorname{ker} (L)=\{ \mathbf{0} \}
$$
